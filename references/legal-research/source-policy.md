# Official-source legal research standard

Each legal proposition MUST have: jurisdiction, public authority/publisher, primary text or official binding guidance URL, law/regulation or section identifier, consultation date (ISO 8601), effect on the observed project, applicability reasoning and uncertainty. Prefer the enacted/operative version and check whether provisions are amended, pending or repealed. Record version dates separately from search dates.

Hierarchy: official statute/regulation and gazette → official regulator or court guidance when relevant → authoritative government implementation notes. A competitor's privacy notice is **not** a legal source and must never be copied as law. Secondary sources may suggest leads but cannot replace official verification.

Examples of starting points only (check current authority, URL and text live):
- Ecuador: Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales: https://www.spdp.gob.ec/
- EU: EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/ ; European Data Protection Board: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/
- US: Federal Trade Commission: https://www.ftc.gov/ ; state legislatures and regulators for state law.
- UK: Information Commissioner's Office: https://ico.org.uk/ ; legislation.gov.uk for enacted texts.
- Brazil: ANPD: https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br

Never assume these URLs alone determine applicability. Never rely on a stale canned list as globally complete. Search country/region official sources for each confirmed market and activity. Record citations in a private research ledger, not in a publicly served page by default.

If browse tools are not available, state `legal-research-unverified`, stop affected legal drafting and request an explicit user decision. Do not substitute memory.
